## Mission

Describe the US Federal Budget visually with maximum clarity and context for average citizens. A tool for clarity that explains, that is ALWAYS sourced. Eventually a hub for exploring votes/amendments — documented sources for understanding spending decisions. Never change the data; never editorialize.

## Ground Rules (set by Chris 2026-06-12 — also in auto-memory: federal-tracker-ground-rules)

1. **No paid APIs** — all data sources free. LLM spend under the existing $5 approval gate.
2. **Sonnet-delegable task list** — mechanical work routed to Sonnet sessions; design/IA judgment stays with the stronger model.
3. **NEVER create or display unsourced data.** Sourcing-throughout is the future direction for ALL of Treasury Tracker.
4. **No reflexive 3-4 level icicles.** Understand the data first, then choose the visualization that best explains it.
5. **New explainer fidelity standard**: plain-language program cost explanations PLUS an expanded "details" section with sourced program/tax origins (enabling bill, public law number, author/sponsor, supporters) from official records — never from model memory.
6. **Safety line**: official public record only (votes, sponsorships, enacted text). Nothing illegal; nothing compromising the safety of officials — no personal info, no addresses, no targeting.
7. **Transparency about opacity**: where the budget isn't auditable (DoD failed audits), say so explicitly with the GAO/OIG citation.

## Key IA Insight — federal is NOT a scaled-up city

- **First split: Mandatory / Discretionary / Net Interest** (~60/27/13). The federal budget's defining fact; explains why "just cut spending" debates concern a minority of the budget and why interest now rivals defense.
- **Two lenses, citizen's choice**: by function (what it's for) and by agency (who spends it). OMB data supports both.
- **Money In has structure**: payroll taxes earmarked (Social Security/Medicare) vs income taxes general fund — explains Social Security's political distinctness structurally.
- **Deficit/debt as first-class context** (receipts ~$4.9T vs outlays ~$6.8T-range — recollection only; recon pins sourced figures).
- **Comparative-scale aids**: per-capita, per-taxpayer, % of total — arithmetic on sourced numbers only.
- **Outlays vs budget authority**: pick outlays (money actually spent) consistently; document the choice visibly.
- Instinct (decide after recon): proportional first-split view → drill into either lens, rather than one deep tree.

## Sourcing Architecture (the big upgrade over enrichCategories.js)

LLM text from model memory is NOT sourced. New standard:
- **Every figure** carries a source chip: dataset name, fetch date, direct URL.
- **Tier 1 explainer**: model summarizes *fetched authoritative text* (agency budget justifications, CBO descriptions) for clarity; citation displayed.
- **Tier 2 details (origins)**: enabling statute, public law number, sponsor, year, cosponsors, amendments — structured data from Congress.gov/GovInfo, every claim linked. Official acts only.
- **Opacity handling**: unauditable portions flagged in UI with the official citation ("we can't show you this, and here's the official record of why").
- Schema implications: `source_url`/`source_date` on enrichment rows; likely a new `program_details` table. Once proven federally, backfills to cities.
